**A clock that was ahead no longer freezes an intent.** A production audit found that one event stamped in the future (a device clock that was wrong for a moment) held an intent until the clock caught up: decay stopped, because the anchor kept the later time; the open episode never timed out, so learning stopped; and a reveal or outcome stamped in the future kept the gate shut until that date plus the cooldown. Every write now moves its anchor to the event's own time, even when that is earlier: the evidence is held (no decay for a backwards step, as before) and decay resumes from the new time, for `updatedAt`, the learned counts' `learnedAt` and the episode's `lastEventAt`. A reveal or outcome stamped ahead of the clock counts as having happened now: `predict` reports `suppressedUntil` one cooldown after the current time rather than years ahead, and the next event pins the stored time to its own. `markRevealed` follows the same rule and trusts the time it is given, so an earlier `at` moves the cooldown start back. What moves for a clock that only ever runs forward: nothing. What moves for one that jumps backwards: the anchor now follows it, so a log replayed with timestamps out of order decays slightly more than before. `decayFactor` of an infinite gap is now 0, not 1. An episode held under `attribution` with a close stamped in the future is left as it was.
