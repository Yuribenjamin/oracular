**The clock rule: one glitch cannot move the engine.** 0.10.0 made every write trust the latest event's time, so that one event stamped in the future could no longer hold an intent until the clock caught up. A production re-test found the cost: a clock that steps *backwards* (a phone that wakes up in 1970 before it syncs, a seconds-for-milliseconds bug) pulled every anchor back with it, and the next event decayed the whole gap again and closed the session, recording a miss nobody earned. The two cases are one ambiguity: after one backward step the engine cannot tell whether the new stamp or the old anchor is the wrong clock. It now waits one event. The engine keeps a clock, the latest event time it trusts; an event stamped behind it counts now, at the clock's time, and its stamp is held. If the next event is also behind the clock, the clock was corrected: every anchor ahead of the held stamp (evidence, learned counts, reveals, outcomes, the open episode, the episodes waiting under `attribution`) moves back to it, and decay and cooldowns resume from there. If the next event is at or after the clock, the held stamp was a glitch, and nothing moved. Monotone clocks see no difference at all, and every number in the docs is unchanged. What changes: a clock that was ahead heals two events after it is corrected rather than one, and heals every intent at once rather than only the ones later events touch; a reveal stamped ahead of the clock is pinned by the next event on any intent; a single backward stamp wipes nothing and closes nothing.

Two costs, stated. The first event after a correction is processed at the uncorrected time, so the intents it touches are decayed to that time and their episodes waiting under `attribution` can settle as misses before the second event moves everything back. And two bad stamps in a row read as a correction, which costs exactly what 0.10 paid on every backward step: the anchors move to the bad time and the next good event decays the gap again. One case no rule covers: a device that boots in 1970 and syncs forward later makes one monotone jump of decades, indistinguishable from a long absence, so hold `observe` until the device clock is trusted.

**State version 4.** The clock is stored, so `STATE_VERSION` is 4 and version 3 states (0.6.0 to 0.11.x) migrate on load: the clock is set to the latest time the stored anchors were written at, nothing is held, and no estimate changes. Under rule 5 of the stability policy, 0.11 reads a version 4 state as its own; what it loses is the clock: a backward stamp awaiting confirmation is forgotten, and the next event is trusted the way 0.11 trusted every event.
